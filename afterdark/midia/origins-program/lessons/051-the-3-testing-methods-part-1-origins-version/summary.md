## Overview
This lesson outlines three ad-testing methodologies—the Marksman Method, the Sniper Method, and the Shotgun Method—aimed at brands making under $100k per month [00:00:11]. The instructor teaches how to translate avatar research into structured ad concepts inside Meta, specifically matching testing formats to creative types (images vs. videos) [00:05:20, 00:12:16]. The goal is to establish rapid, avatar-aware feedback loops to identify winning angles and scale efficiently without wasting time on ineffective single-variable testing [00:14:20, 00:16:13].

## Key teachings
- Avatar awareness is the foundation of testing; regardless of the method used, advertisers must understand which avatar and angle a winning ad connects with rather than focusing purely on structural ad metrics [00:01:08, 00:15:12].
- Perfect single-variable testing is nearly impossible under Meta's current algorithm because small swings are ignored or grouped together by the platform [00:14:20, 00:19:57].
- The Marksman Method should be used primarily for image ads to test multiple angles simultaneously and quickly find a winning direction [00:05:20, 00:08:09].
- The Sniper Method is used to go deeper into a validated direction (or when testing videos) by executing a single angle across multiple variations [00:09:30, 00:12:16].
- When an image ad angle finds traction (spend or purchases), translating that angle into a video via the Sniper Method typically scales significantly better [00:11:40].
- Creative strategy teams must not use the Shotgun Method internally by generating micro-iterations with AI or slight hook variations, as Meta views them as identical ads [00:18:14, 00:19:51].
- The Shotgun Method should be reserved strictly for high-volume creator content (e.g., TikTok Shop or product seeding/UGC), where account traction is analyzed retroactively to extract angles for Sniper/Marksman testing [00:16:56, 00:20:06].
- Strong sub-avatar and angle alignment outweighs copywriting perfection; even mediocre AI copy can convert if the angle is deeply relatable [00:15:57].

## Frameworks and models
### The 3 Testing Methods
1. **Marksman Method** [00:02:16]
   - *Definition:* Testing multiple angles within a single concept to find a viable direction without over-restricting creativity [00:02:20, 00:03:21].
   - *Application:* Used primarily for static image testing [00:05:20].
   - *Structure:* 1 Concept containing 3 variations, where each variation tests a completely different angle with 1 chosen hook/headline [00:06:55, 00:08:32].
2. **Sniper Method** [00:09:24]
   - *Definition:* Testing within a strict environment to validate a specific angle by trying multiple execution styles/hooks [00:09:30, 00:10:52].
   - *Application:* Used primarily for video ads and iterating on angles that gained spend during Marksman testing [00:11:21, 00:12:16].
   - *Structure:* 1 Concept focused on 1 Angle, using 3 different variations (hooks, formats, or visual styles) [00:10:52].
3. **Shotgun Method** [00:16:38]
   - *Definition:* Launching large volumes of un-strategized content to see what hits [00:16:46].
   - *Application:* Content strategy only (TikTok Shop content, Insense seeding, UGC influx) [00:16:56]. Once an angle gains spend, it transitions into the Marksman or Sniper workflows [00:17:24].

### Testing Lifecycle Workflow
The instructor defines the agency workflow across three cyclical stages [00:11:35]:
1. **Step 1:** Use the **Marksman Method** first to find the direction of winning angles [00:11:35].
2. **Step 2:** Use the **Sniper Method** second to go deeper and find winners once a direction has been found [00:11:35].
3. **Step 3:** Return to the **Marksman Method** when performance plateaus on that angle [00:11:35].

### Core Definitions
- **3:2:2:** Meta testing structure consisting of 3 creatives, 2 body copies, and 2 headlines within 1 concept [00:07:03, 00:07:40].
- **Concept / Batch / Test #:** The tracking number for a test (e.g., Batch #1, Batch #29, Batch #133) [00:07:10].
- **Concept / Swing:** The overarching testing strategy or big idea (e.g., "Test superlative angles," "Test different avatars") [00:07:10].
- **Angle:** The selling strategy and primary reason given to buy (e.g., "Doctor-designed," "Protect melatonin") [00:07:10].
