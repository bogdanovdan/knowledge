## Overview

This lesson breaks down 13 high-performing static ad concepts and provides customizable Canva templates for each. Aimed at e-commerce brand owners creating Facebook advertising assets, the instructor explains the strategic psychology and messaging principles behind each ad style rather than treating them as blind swipe files.

## Key teachings

## Frameworks and models

### The 13 Winning Static Ad Types

1. **Avatar Call Out** [00:03:40]
   - *Core mechanism*: Clear, direct call-out of a specific target audience in the headline (e.g., "Moms of 15 Year Olds").
   - *Supporting elements*: A real customer review reinforcing the avatar callout, authentic imagery matching the context, and congruent social proof.

2. **Solution Exaggeration** [00:03:50, 00:09:20]
   - *Core mechanism*: Prominent numbers that halt the scroll by highlighting how long or effectively the solution works (e.g., "14+ Hours. No Swelling.").
   - *Supporting elements*: Clear body copy explaining the product mechanism (e.g., compression socks).

3. **Stealing Credibility** [00:10:10]
   - *Core mechanism*: Borrowing recognition from an established, effective mechanism or cultural trend (e.g., "Ozempic's New Bestie").
   - *Supporting elements*: Visual curiosity hooks (e.g., macro patterns like gummy bears) paired with clear nutritional/benefit breakdown blocks.

4. **This Is Not [The Product]** [00:10:15]
   - *Core mechanism*: Wordplay stating the item is not what it appears to be at first glance (e.g., "This is a meal. Not a protein shake.").
   - *Supporting elements*: Establishes a curiosity gap without overstretching believability, reframing the perceived category into a higher-value proposition.

5. **Specific Pain Point** [00:10:40]
   - *Core mechanism*: Targeting precise, visceral problem phrases rather than generic descriptions (e.g., "Lose the muffin top for good" instead of "Lose belly fat").
   - *Supporting elements*: Direct price slash positioning and clear guarantee badges.

6. **Accidental Ideal Outcome** [00:10:50]
   - *Core mechanism*: Leveraging the word "accidentally" to evoke curiosity around an effortless benefit (e.g., "I Accidentally Lost 12 Pounds At 52").
   - *Supporting elements*: Highly visual action copy ("blocking fat cells," "dissolving old fat") and the "Law of Unique Numbers" using non-rounded figures (e.g., 12 pounds, 52 years old, 56.4%).

7. **Relatable Hook** [00:11:15]
   - *Core mechanism*: Directly visualizing a painful physical sensation or relatable situation using an inherently visual product (e.g., "12-Hour Shift Shoes" for healthcare workers).
   - *Supporting elements*: Story-driven, customer-focused copy leading to a long-form advertorial.

8. **Indirect Avatar** [00:11:35]
   - *Core mechanism*: Calling out the buyer by referencing the person they care for or deal with (e.g., "Teen Always Grumpy?" targeting stressed parents).
   - *Supporting elements*: Educational angle introducing a biological/chemical root cause (deficiencies), paired with product ingredient breakdowns.

9. **The Free Ad** [00:11:55]
   - *Core mechanism*: Strong, front-facing value offer (e.g., "Get 4 Free Gifts") appealing to high-intent, product-aware audiences.
   - *Supporting elements*: Outcome-focused bullet points centered on desired transformation.

10. **Classy Offer** [00:12:00]
    - *Core mechanism*: Presenting promotional sales or BOGO offers with elevated, minimal aesthetics rather than aggressive discount styling (e.g., "Left or right? Just add both to cart. One's on us").

11. **Simplified Us vs Them** [00:12:20]
    - *Core mechanism*: Contrasting an ordinary benchmark against the branded product in minimal words (e.g., "This is bread. This is breakfast.").

12. **Pubity Style** [00:12:25]
    - *Core mechanism*: Mimicking social media news/editorial aggregation posts to camouflage into the user feed.
    - *Supporting elements*: Small circular image inset combined with a dramatic headline and newsworthy angle tied to product protection.

13. **Minimal Us vs Them** [00:13:10]
    - *Core mechanism*: Inverting traditional comparison tables by listing pain points on the competitor ("Them") side, and countering with simple elimination copy on the product side (e.g., "None of that.").

## Numbers, benchmarks and rules of thumb

- **90-day filter**: Winning status is evaluated based on ads active and generating high engagement/shares over the preceding 90 days [00:02:41].
- **Scaling threshold**: The Pubity-style ad format has scaled to "a few thousand dollars a day" in ad spend for the instructor's brand portfolio [00:12:29].
- **Law of Unique Numbers**: Using precise, unrounded numbers (such as 12 pounds, age 52, or 56.4%) increases audience believability compared to rounded figures like 10, 50, or 60% [00:10:50].
