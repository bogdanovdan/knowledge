## Overview
This lesson breaks down the primary e-commerce offer structures that brands can run to scale ad spend, lift average order value (AOV), and improve conversion rates. Aimed at e-commerce brand owners looking to move from $0 to $100k/month, the instructor details specific offer mechanics, fulfillment margin optimizations, and the psychology of framing discounts.

## Key teachings
- Calculate your new break-even ROAS across every single offer before launching, especially when structuring multi-offer campaigns [00:00:08].
- Bundle & Save is the highest-leverage offer because it enables discounting without devaluing the core product, increases AOV, and capitalizes on combined shipping efficiencies [00:00:29], [00:02:17].
- When bundling physical items, negotiate with fulfillment partners to pack small items in the same box at no extra shipping cost to protect COGS [00:02:24].
- Multi-pack bundles require logical alignment: consumable products or essentials (e.g., t-shirts, supplements) work as multi-packs, whereas single-need products require complementary add-ons or gift framing [00:03:32], [00:04:19].
- Offering store credit (e.g., a $20–$30 gift card delivered via email) functions as an effective bundle incentive because approximately 80% of consumers never redeem it [00:05:12], [00:05:24].
- Follow the "law of large numbers" when displaying discounts: show the larger numeral between the dollar discount and the percentage discount [00:07:27], [00:08:06].
- If an item has a low AOV, do not sell single units; enforce a minimum order quantity (e.g., minimum buy of 5 via a Buy 2 Get 3 Free offer) to make economics work [00:08:56], [00:09:07].
- When margins do not allow "Buy X Get X Free," switch to "Buy X Get X% Off" (e.g., Buy 1, Get 2nd at 50% off) or implement conditional thresholds in the cart [00:11:04], [00:11:31].

## Frameworks and models
### Offer Archetypes Classification
1. **Bundle & Save (Highly Recommended)** [00:00:29]
   - *Mechanic:* Group commonly purchased products at a bundled price.
   - *Requirements:* Give the bundle a clear name and a specific reason for existing.
   - *Execution variations:* Same-SKU quantity tiers (e.g., 6-pack shirts) or hero SKU + complementary accessories + digital bonus (e.g., eBook guide).
2. **$X Amount Off** [00:06:48]
   - *Mechanic:* Flat dollar discount off a product or minimum order amount.
   - *Best used for:* Higher AOV products where the absolute dollar figure is visually larger than the percentage figure.
3. **%X Amount Off** [00:08:19]
   - *Mechanic:* Percentage discount off product or order.
   - *Best used for:* Lower AOV products where the percentage number exceeds the dollar value (e.g., 30% off vs. $9 off).
4. **Buy X Get X Free** [00:08:39]
   - *Mechanic:* Customer purchases a threshold quantity and receives additional units free.
   - *Variations:* "Buy 1 Get 1 Free," "Buy 2 Get 1 Free," "Buy 2 Get 3 Free."
5. **Buy X Get X% Off (For Low-Margin Catalogs)** [00:11:04]
   - *Mechanic:* Purchase threshold triggers a partial discount on additional units.
   - *Variations:* "Buy 1 Get 1 50% Off," progressive cart drawer tier bars.
6. **Incentive Add-Ons (Gifts & Shipping)** [00:12:02]
   - *Mechanic:* Stacking free e-books, mystery gifts, store credit gift cards, or conditional free shipping thresholds onto existing offers.

## Numbers, benchmarks and rules of thumb
- **80%**: Estimated rate of customers who never redeem issued store credit gift cards [00:05:24].
- **Law of Large Numbers cutoff**: Use % for low AOV (e.g., 30% off beats $9 off) and $ for high AOV (e.g., $20 off beats 5% off) [00:07:37], [00:08:13].
- **10% to 15% off**: Simple baseline discount that frequently drives top-performing landing page conversions [00:08:24].
- **Under $5 shipping fee**: Price threshold where charging shipping does not hurt conversions [00:12:24].
