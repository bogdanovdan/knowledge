## Overview
This lesson outlines a 14-question checklist used by the agency to review and critique video ad briefs and finished creatives. Aimed at e-commerce advertisers and media buyers seeking to scale winning video ads, the framework is designed to diagnose why an ad failed to convert or to evaluate an ad brief before launching.

## Key teachings
- A high-performing video ad checklist can be applied either before submitting an ad brief/launching, or after testing when an ad fails to convert or scale [00:00:23].
- Media buying mechanics matter very little compared to creative; scalable results can be achieved using a single campaign [00:01:18].
- The first 1–2 seconds must not only hook the viewer visually or emotionally (like putting a lit firecracker in your pocket) but also retain them [00:01:44].
- Creative evaluation must prioritize what the customer actually wants to see on their feed over what the creator personally likes [00:02:42].
- Effective copywriting creates strong, instant mental imagery (e.g., comparing earwax volume to *Shrek* making candles) [00:03:07].
- Ads should feel native to the specific platform (e.g., Instagram/Facebook) rather than looking like high-production television or Netflix commercials [00:03:49].
- Identity labels (e.g., "marketing nerd," "dropshipper," "last-minute person") build instant relatability with the target audience [00:04:22].
- An ad does not need to satisfy all 14 questions; for instance, an ad can rely on humor, pure creative education, or camouflage [00:05:15].
- Camouflaged ads—creatives that blend into the feed, look authentic, and do not immediately feel like ads—work well when targeting problem-aware or unaware audiences [00:05:55].
- When incorporating past winning ad elements, extract the underlying customer insight or benefit rather than mechanically copying the exact hook format, avoiding "iteration overload" [00:06:55].
- Direct statements of desires fall flat; core desires must be framed creatively (e.g., "Here's how to get her to never stop texting you") [00:07:40].
- Escalation—humorously exaggerating a product claim to its extreme (e.g., a humidifier "cleaner than Mr. Clean" or hair supplements turning someone into Chewbacca)—is an effective creative device popularized by agencies like Harmon Brothers [00:08:53].
- Advertisers should carve out a "blue ocean" by developing unique mechanisms and USPs rather than directly copying competitor creatives, establishing a brand moat [00:10:24].

## Frameworks and models
### 14 Questions to Review Video Ads
The instructor presents the following sequential questions to audit ad briefs and video creatives [00:00:55]:
1. Does the first 1–2 seconds of this ad hook the RIGHT user in & KEEP them hooked in?
2. Is this a video that your potential customers would want to see?
3. Does the copywriting create a clear image / visual in the user's mind?
4. Does this video feel native to the platform?
5. Does this video contain a label for who the type of person is?
6. Would this video make someone smile or laugh?
7. Is the video relatable?
8. Does this video actually look like an ad?
9. Gut check – does this video feel authentic?
10. What learnings of previous winning ads have we applied to this ad?
11. Does the ad educate the user in a creative way on how to achieve their core desire?
12. Does this video tell a story your ideal customer would find interesting?
13. Have you applied escalation to exaggerate your claim in a clear and relatable way?
14. Does this ad separate your brand from the competition?
