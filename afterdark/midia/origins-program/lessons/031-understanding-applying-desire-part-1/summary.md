## Overview
This lesson breaks down the role of human desires in building customer avatars and writing ad copy for Shopify e-commerce brands. It explains why defining audiences by surface demographics fails, and teaches how to connect permanent biological instincts and technological frustrations with cultural trends and mass education. The material is aimed at media buyers and e-commerce founders seeking to produce higher-converting ad headlines and elevate products into higher-ticket, emotionally urgent market categories.

## Key teachings
- Mass desire is defined as "the public spread of a private want"—when millions privately want the same thing, advertising can harness it [00:01:10].
- Defining avatars by demographics alone is ineffective; a 22-year-old and a 45-year-old frequently share the exact same desire (e.g., to make money or find love) [00:02:05].
- The core rule of advertising is that you cannot create desire; you can only channel existing desires onto your product [00:03:29].
- Advertisers can uncover core desires by repeatedly asking "why" something matters until reaching the underlying human instinct [00:04:22].
- The permanent human drive for survival and status existed 10,000 years ago, exists today, and will exist in 10,000 years; only the mechanisms used to satiate those desires change [00:10:20].
- Technology inherently creates recurring problems across eras (bad TV reception in the 1960s, unstable internet today, lagging holograms in 2050), which continuously generate new consumer desires [00:17:59].
- Tapping into a mass instinct combined with mass education typically yields the biggest ad creative breakthroughs compared to other combinations [00:25:27].
- Copy iterations must not alter the underlying desire accidentally; changing a health-focused angle like "Why are you still choking your dog in 2025?" to a status-focused angle like "Why are you still wearing collars in 2025?" shifts the emotional drive and impacts performance [00:59:46].
- Execution determines outcomes: having the right desire is insufficient if the copywriting fails to communicate and channel it effectively [01:01:17].

## Frameworks and models

### The 3 Forces of Desire (Spencer's Edition) [00:04:53]
1. **Permanent Forces (The Things People Will Always Have)**
   - **Mass Instincts** (Universal human drives):
     - *Health*: Survival, staying alive, staying healthy, and safe [00:07:31].
     - *Sex*: Being attractive, finding a mate, feeling desired, intimacy [00:07:39].
     - *Status*: Being important, respected, better than others, gaining visible recognition [00:08:03].
     - *Belonging*: Fitting in, having friends, avoiding rejection, family/tribe association [00:08:25].
     - *Control*: Having power, predicting outcomes, being in charge, resisting unwanted changes [00:09:00].
     - *Comfort*: Making life easier, avoiding physical/mental pain, avoiding unnecessary work [00:09:25].
   - **Mass Technological Problems** (Universal friction caused by tools/advancements):
     - *Complexity*: Too hard to operate; instructions fail (triggers feeling: **dumb**) [00:14:31].
     - *Overwhelm*: Too many choices, alerts, notifications (triggers feeling: **anxious**) [00:15:08].
     - *Fragility*: Breaks down right when needed most (triggers feeling: **frustrated**) [00:15:44].
     - *Maintenance*: Constant updates, recurring subscription fees, repairs (triggers feeling: **tired**) [00:16:09].
     - *Incompatibility*: Products fail to connect or integrate with existing setups (triggers feeling: **trapped**) [00:16:53].
     - *Obsolescence*: Tools quickly become outdated or unsupported (triggers feeling: **cheated**) [00:17:12].
2. **Forces of Change (What Changes How Permanent Desires Are Fulfilled)** [00:18:41]
   - **Style Trends**: What is currently fashionable or culturally valued (e.g., focus/commitment culture, biohacking, built-to-last products).
   - **Mass Education**: What society learns over time (e.g., medical studies on collar tracheal damage, sleep cycle light disruption, GLP-1 weight loss mechanisms).

### 3-Step Process to Find & Evaluate Desires [00:35:00]
1. **Spot the Desire**: Monitor complaint patterns, search for explicit "wants" and "needs," track cultural trends, and analyze where consumers spend money.
2. **Test Its Power (The 3 Dimensions)**:
   - *Scope*: How many people share this desire? (e.g., fine antiques vs. making money) [00:39:39].
   - *Urgency*: How desperately do they need immediate relief? (e.g., broken bone vs. minor cut) [00:36:55].
   - *Staying Power*: Does the desire renew continuously? (e.g., hunger/sex vs. a one-time trip to Paris) [00:38:34].
3. **Choose the Strongest**: Select the desire scoring highest across the 3 dimensions using the instinct power rankings hierarchy.

### Instinct Power Rankings [00:40:33]
- **#1 Health**: Ultimate emotional trigger; survival threats compel action across all income brackets.
- **#2 Status**: Heavily amplified by social media; competition and visibility drive multi-billion-dollar markets.
- **#3 Sex**: Biological imperative, fundamental yet frequently communicated subtly due to ad platform policies.
- **#4 Comfort**: Elevated by modern technology; consumers expect reduced friction.
- **#5 Control**: Triggered by modern complexity; consumers seek order over chaotic systems.
- **#6 Belonging**: Critical, but less survival-urgent in modern society than raw health or status.

### The Elevation Strategy [00:44:40]
A positioning model where products are shifted upward along the instinct hierarchy to increase perceived value, scale, and emotional urgency.
- **Moving Up**: Shifts products from commodity utility (e.g., comfort/control) to high-emotion categories (status/health).
- **Moving Down Risk**: Repositioning downwards reduces perceived value, softens emotional urgency, lowers pricing power, and forces the brand to compete in commodity categories (e.g., marketing a Rolex watch as "convenient timekeeping" [Comfort] instead of "Status") [00:46:07].
- **Authenticity Rule**: Elevation must feel natural and logical; forcing a connection (e.g., claiming notebook paper attracts romantic partners) causes consumers to detect manipulation [00:47:32].

#### Product Hierarchy Application: Dog Harness [00:48:39]
- *Level 6 (Belonging - Weakest)*: "Join the responsible dog owner community." / "Every caring dog owner needs this."
- *Level 5 (Comfort - Weak)*: "No more struggling with your dog" / "Finally, stress-free dog walks"
- *Level 4 (Control - Moderate)*: "Take control of every walk" / "Master your dog's pulling instantly"
- *Level 3 (Sex - Strong)*: "She'll ask where you got that harness" / "Turn heads on every walk"
- *Level 2 (Status - Very Strong)*: "Why celebrity trainers won't use anything else" / "The harness only 3% of dog owners know about"
- *Level 1 (Health - Strongest)*: "Why vets are begging owners to switch" / "Why are you still choking your dog in 2025?"
