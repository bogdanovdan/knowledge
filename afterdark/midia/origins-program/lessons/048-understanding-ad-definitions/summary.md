## Key teachings
- Standardizing definitions is necessary to think less about terminology philosophies and focus entirely on ad execution [00:01:15].
- The testing framework used inside Meta is "3:2:2", which consists of 3 creatives, 2 primary body copies, and 2 headlines [00:01:29].
- Every single ad requires an explicit angle (the reason why someone buys); ad creative often fails when creators test generic formats or concepts (like "Us vs. Them" or "UGC") without defining what is actually being sold [00:06:35].
- A Concept (or Swing) represents the overall test strategy or big idea (e.g., testing an iteration, testing a format, testing avatars), whereas an Angle represents the selling strategy (the customer-facing reason to buy) [00:04:18, [00:05:37]].
- If the concept itself is to test a specific angle (e.g., "Test durability angle"), the concept effectively matches the angle; however, best practice requires writing the angle down separately to ensure clear tracking and customer focus [00:08:25].
- Variations represent the 3 individual creative executions of a concept; these can be different ways of phrasing a single angle, or tests of different angles within a broader concept [00:11:13, [00:12:33]].
- Beginners are often recommended to test multiple angles across their variations within a broader concept test [00:11:43].

## Frameworks and models

### The 6 Core Meta Ad Definitions
The instructor breaks down ad organization into six sequential terms [00:15:23]:

1. **3:2:2** [00:01:29]
   - *Technical definition:* 3 creatives, 2 body copies, and 2 headlines.
   - *12-year-old definition:* The testing structure used inside Meta.
   - *Structure:* The entire setup constitutes one Concept. The "3" are variations of that concept; the "2:2" are the copy elements placed in Meta.

2. **Concept / Batch / Test #** [00:02:26]
   - *Technical definition:* The tracking number for the test.
   - *12-year-old definition:* The number used to keep track of which test you are on.
   - *Examples:* Batch #1, Batch #29, Batch #133.

3. **Concept / Swing** [00:03:33]
   - *Technical definition:* The test strategy—what is being tested in this specific batch.
   - *12-year-old definition:* The big idea behind the ads you want to test.
   - *Examples:* "Test an iteration on a winning video", "Test superlative angles", "Test UGC format", "Test different avatars".

4. **Angle** [00:05:33]
   - *Technical definition:* The selling strategy—how you choose to sell the product.
   - *12-year-old definition:* The main reason you give someone to buy.
   - *Examples:* "Buy It For Life", "Postpartum weight despite trying everything", "Stainless steel / Doctor-designed / Cleanest".

5. **Variations** [00:11:13]
   - *Technical definition:* Individual executions of the angle(s).
   - *12-year-old definition:* Different ways to communicate your selling reason(s).
   - *Single-angle execution example:* Angle is "Buy It For Life" -> Variations: "One harness, lifetime of adventures", "Buy it once, adventure for life", or "End the replacement cycle".
   - *Multi-angle execution example:* Concept is "Superlative angles" -> Variations: "Stainless steel", "Doctor-designed", and "Easiest to clean".

6. **Format** [00:13:13]
   - *Technical definition:* How the ad is delivered.
   - *12-year-old definition:* The type of ad you are making.
   - *High-level types:* Image, video, carousel, GIF.
   - *Specific executions:* Video with a real person talking, slideshow of pictures, product image, AI voice-overs, or UGC.
