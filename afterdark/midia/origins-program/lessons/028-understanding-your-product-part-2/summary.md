## Overview
This lesson demonstrates how to manually complete the "Product Overview Document" when you have not conducted prior customer research and only have access to a basic product page. Using the e-commerce brand Dog Friendly Co. as a live working example, the instructor walks through breaking down a product from basic specifications into features, assumed benefits, underlying desires, and unique or hidden mechanisms. The training is aimed at Shopify brand owners and marketers looking to deeply understand their product to generate winning ad hooks and avatar profiles [00:00:00].

## Key teachings
- Most consumers read at a 6th-grade level; marketing copy must be simplified to avoid overly technical or high-level jargon [00:01:23].
- Product features must always be translated into benefits (what the feature does) and then into desires (what emotional or functional outcome the customer actually wants) [00:06:33, [00:14:24]].
- Deep desires are reached by repeatedly asking the question: *"Why does someone want/need that benefit?"* [00:14:32, [00:18:35]].
- Breaking down features and benefits naturally surfaces viable angle ideas and storytelling concepts for ads (e.g., custom name patches preventing dog loss or military buckles preventing breakages during emergencies) [00:11:11, [00:20:41]].
- AI tools like Claude should be used as collaborative brainstorming partners rather than complete replacements, because AI lacks context on your exact current advertising unless prompted carefully [00:14:10, [00:25:05]].
- Prompting AI to explain a product "as if telling a friend" or asking "what would customers complain about if it worked too well" helps uncover conversational, non-salesy ad copy and unexpected angles [00:27:00, [00:30:24]].

## Frameworks and models
### The Product Overview Document Process
A 7-step worksheet to extract advertising hooks and avatar data from any product [00:00:45, [00:32:09]]:

1. **Step 1: Basics**
   - Record the Product Name and Price Point (including sale pricing/slashes) [00:00:47].
2. **Step 2: Product In Action**
   - Describe what the product does, how it works, and the outcome it provides [00:01:05].
   - Translate the explanation into a 6th-grade reading level version using readability checks [00:01:15].
3. **Step 3: Features**
   - List the physical components, specifications, materials, or distinct design elements (aim for 4–5 core features) [00:05:42].
4. **Step 4: Assumed Benefits**
   - For each listed feature, answer: *What does it DO for the customer?* (One feature can produce multiple distinct benefits) [00:07:53, [00:09:34]].
5. **Step 5: Assumed Desires**
   - For each benefit, answer: *Why does someone want or need that benefit?* Map benefits directly to human motivations (e.g., safety, avoiding embarrassment, ease of use, patriotism) [00:14:24].
6. **Step 6: Assumed New Mechanisms (USPs)**
   - Identify what makes the product unique or what mechanism separates it from competing alternatives on the market [00:21:14].
7. **Step 7: Hidden Mechanisms (For AI Analysis)**
   - Answer 4 creative diagnostic questions to surface blind spots [00:24:12, [00:28:00]]:
     1. *What does your product do that you DON'T advertise?*
     2. *What ingredients/components do you include that competitors don't?*
     3. *If your product worked TOO well, what would be the "problem" customers would complain about?*
     4. *If someone was explaining this product to their friend, what would they say?*
