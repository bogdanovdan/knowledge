## Overview
This lesson demonstrates how to construct specific, actionable sub-avatars from a single desire-based core avatar using Reddit research. Geared toward e-commerce founders and marketers scaling from $0 to $100k/month, the training explains how sub-avatars form the foundation for marketing angles, hooks, and creative ad copy.

## Key teachings
- Every avatar and sub-avatar must have a desire attached to it, because desire is the underlying force that compels customers to make a purchase [00:01:13].
- A core avatar should be broad enough to branch out into multiple sub-avatars, but must not be as broad as a primal core human desire (e.g., choose "wants restorative sleep" instead of just "wants to be healthy") [00:08:12].
- The product and its mechanism must realistically deliver on the core avatar's desire; marketing an implausible connection or failing to deliver on the promise kills customer retention and brand viability [00:05:38].
- Once a single desire-based core avatar is selected, sub-avatars are generated across the remaining four categories: experiences (including product experiences), emotions, behaviors, and demographics [00:13:16].
- Behavior is the most effective angle category for ad relatability because everyday actions cut deep and resonate immediately with audiences [00:10:22].
- Customer language discovered in Reddit threads can be turned directly into high-converting ad hooks and angles using AI tools like Claude [00:18:02].
- Sub-avatars directly generate relatable marketing angles; stronger, more specific sub-avatars produce better ad performance [00:32:11].

## Frameworks and models
### The 5 Core Categories Framework
Used to map customer research into structured core avatars and sub-avatars [00:13:16]:
1. **Desire:** The primary outcome the customer wants to achieve (e.g., wants restorative sleep).
2. **Experience / Product Experience:** Prior attempts, past product usage, or recurring situations (e.g., tried magnesium and failed, tracks sleep via Whoop/Oura, wakes up 3–4 times per night).
3. **Emotion:** Internal feelings and mental states caused by the problem (e.g., feels on edge, moody, guilty, anxious, overwhelmed).
4. **Behavior:** Observable physical actions and daily routines (e.g., wears nose strips, avoids food 3 hours before bed, leaves windows open to freeze the room).
5. **Demographics:** External characteristics such as age, profession, and schedule (e.g., 60+ early nighters, night shift/graveyard workers).

### How Desires Are Born: The 2 Forces
Classification of foundational drives behind consumer motivation [00:08:40]:
1. **Permanent Forces (Mass Instincts):** Universal drives that work across all human history:
   - *Health:* Survival, staying alive and safe.
   - *Sex:* Being attractive, finding a mate, feeling desired.
   - *Status:* Being important, respected, superior to others.
   - *Belonging:* Fitting in, having friends, avoiding rejection.
   - *Control:* Having power, predicting outcomes, being in charge.
   - *Comfort:* Making life easier, avoiding pain and work.
2. **Mass Technological Problems:** Problems created by evolving technology across eras.

### Sub-Avatar Development Process
1. **Pick One Broader Surface Desire:** Establish the central anchor (e.g., "Wants restorative sleep").
2. **Mine Customer Forums:** Search Google and Reddit for problem phrases matching the other four categories.
3. **Extract Relatable Hooks & Labels:** Note the community's own language (e.g., "night shifters," "vampire lifestyle," "waking up 3–4 times for no reason").
4. **Draft Specific Sub-Avatars:** Combine the core desire with 1–2 specific traits (behaviors, past product failures, or demographic settings).
5. **Generate Creative Angles:** Translate customer quotes into hooks using direct contrast, problem agitation, or callouts.

## Numbers, benchmarks and rules of thumb
