## Key teachings
- Ad formats describe structural composition (e.g., footage type, text overlay, editing style), not angles or concepts [00:00:11].
- Voiceover with B-roll uses spoken narration over product shots or stock footage, paired with low-volume music that sets the pace [00:00:26].
- Subtitles with B-roll skip voiceover and rely on text and emotive background music to let viewers read in their own internal voice [00:01:00].
- Slideshow videos with text allow brands with minimal or no original video footage to animate static images with slow zooms and text reveals [00:01:46].
- Authentic talking-head UGC relies on an unpolished, direct-to-camera delivery with minimal editing; the instructor cautions that their team avoids AI personas for talking-head ads [00:02:24].
- High-production studio video shoots require significant effort and budget but frequently fail to convert for performance marketing, though they can work at higher scale [00:03:17].
- Never fake press releases or news articles (e.g., USA Today layouts) in native-style image ads; doing so constitutes false advertising and carries legal risk [00:06:17].
- Curated publisher meme styles (e.g., Pubity layout) are acceptable native creative styles because they are recognizable social formats rather than fake news reports [00:06:33].
- Meme-style ads typically produce very low CPCs and high engagement early on to combat high CPMs, but they generally yield lower overall conversion rates [00:08:26].
- Beginners should prioritize launching static image formats before moving into video production [00:10:00].

## Frameworks and models
### The Meta-First Creative Format Taxonomy
The instructor divides creative formats into two main branches:

#### 1. Video Formats
- **Voiceover with B-roll** [00:00:26]: Real or AI voiceover layered over product shots, A-roll, stock, or UGC footage; low-volume background music.
- **Subtitles with B-roll** [00:00:55]: Text-only captions overlaid on footage with background music, forcing the viewer to use internal dialogue.
- **Slideshow Video with Text** [00:01:46]: Static photos spliced with movement (slow zoom-in/zoom-out transitions) and progressive text reveals.
- **Authentic UGC / Talking Head Only** [00:02:24]: Single-take or lightly edited creator speaking directly to the camera with a casual, authentic feel.
- **Professional Shot Video** [00:02:57]: High-production, studio-lit, camera-crew footage with polished branding and overlay steps.
- **Animation** [00:03:46]: 2D/3D motion graphics or character animations used to explain a concept without live humans.

#### 2. Image Formats
- **Static Graphic / Design** [00:04:42]: Branded Canva-style designs featuring bold headlines, product cutouts, and structured value props (e.g., "us vs. them" comparisons, offer callouts).
- **Product Photography + Headline** [00:05:08]: High-resolution studio or lifestyle product imagery paired with a dominant headline and optional feature callouts.
- **Infographic Design** [00:05:26]: Anatomical diagrams, comparative charts, or step-by-step graphic layouts communicating data-driven benefits.
- **Native-Style Image** [00:06:00]: Unbranded, organic-looking social media post styles (e.g., Pubity cards, verified press clipping features).
- **Quote / Text-Heavy Image** [00:06:50]: High-contrast, text-dominant ads (minimal graphics) highlighting an article hook, review quote, or provocative question.
- **Collage Style** [00:07:22]: Grid or scattered multi-image layouts demonstrating multiple benefits, product angles, or contextual settings in a single asset.
- **UGC Image** [00:07:47]: Unpolished customer selfies, real people holding the product, or apparel displayed on real/AI models.
- **Meme-Style Image** [00:08:26]: Widely recognized viral templates customized with brand-related problem-and-solution punchlines.
