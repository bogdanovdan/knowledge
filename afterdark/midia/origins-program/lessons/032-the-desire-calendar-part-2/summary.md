## Overview

This lesson introduces the "Evidence-Based Desire Calendar," a seasonal marketing reference based on consumer behavior and biological shifts (such as sunlight and dopamine levels) across the year. Aimed at e-commerce store owners troubleshooting performance slumps or preparing campaigns, the instructor explains how consumer priorities cycle through core desires—such as Control, Status, Belonging, Comfort, Sex, and Health—so advertisers can tailor ad messaging ahead of seasonal spending shifts.

## Key teachings

- Consumer purchasing psychology changes cyclically due to environmental and biological drivers like sun exposure and dopamine; shoppers make larger lifestyle purchases in summer and frequent emotional or gift-oriented purchases in winter [00:00:52].
- When ad performance dips, brands should consult the Desire Calendar to identify which macro desires are dominating consumer mindsets rather than assuming ads simply stopped working [00:04:43].
- Aligning ad hooks and angles with seasonal macro trends does not mean abandoning winning ads; rather, advertisers should spin off variations aligned with peak desires [00:04:43, 00:16:35].
- Back-to-school buying cycles begin far earlier than September: the majority of shoppers begin purchasing in early July, making late-August and September campaigns too late to capture peak demand [00:07:37].
- Spending naturally constricts in late August and early September as families confront credit card bills and summer travel expenses, prompting a shift toward routine, control, and budget discipline [00:06:12].
- In September, lead advertising angles with Control ("Take charge of your fall routine") and layer Status ("Be the parent/professional that everyone admires") [00:03:14].
- Effective marketing mirrors what prospective buyers are experiencing emotionally in real life (e.g., empty-nest mothers sending kids off to college in September) [00:09:58].
- Holiday and event messaging must launch weeks ahead of the calendar event: consumers buy gifts *for* occasions (like Valentine's Day or Halloween), not *on* the day of the occasion [00:11:24, 00:15:08].
