## Overview
This lesson delivers an in-depth framework for developing actionable customer avatars designed specifically for creative-led Meta advertising. Aimed at e-commerce brand operators seeking to scale past generalized demographic profiles, it explains how Meta targeting is driven by creative relevance rather than ad-set button clicks. The instructor presents a five-category hierarchy—starting with desires, experiences, emotions, and behaviors, and placing demographics last—to build relatable, high-converting core avatars and sub-avatars across different revenue stages.

## Key teachings
- An avatar is a group of specific people most likely to buy your product; the primary objective of an avatar is building instant relatability so prospects think "that's exactly me" [00:02:18], [00:03:08].
- Traditional avatars fail because marketers start with demographics (e.g., age, job title, parental status), resulting in broad profiles that are not actionable when writing ads [00:06:41], [00:08:37].
- Meta targeting is now driven approximately 99% by the creative itself rather than ad-set interest selections; the algorithm rewards relevance with lower costs and higher conversion rates [00:15:30], [00:16:03].
- Common and broad avatars have become highly sophisticated in e-commerce; when targeting broad avatars, success requires a new mechanism, new educational information, or the best offer in the market [00:12:21], [00:14:20].
- Avatars operate along a linear scale ranging from ultra-specific (single-person profile) to completely broad ("you have a heartbeat"), with infinite non-linear combinations along the spectrum [00:18:41], [00:21:00].
- When constructing avatars, always start with high-intent categories (desires, experiences, behaviors) and use demographics last so you do not prematurely alienate potential buyers [00:27:20], [02:08:01].
- Brands earn the right to target broader avatars as their product quality, brand equity, offer strength, and marketing skillset improve [00:30:23], [02:25:24].
- Beginners should strive to "out-avatar" competitors with deeper relatability rather than trying to "out-market" them [02:26:31].
- Core avatars isolate a single category, while sub-avatars combine the core starting category with additional categories to achieve depth [00:24:24], [01:32:57].
- Beliefs should not serve as an independent core avatar category because they represent an amalgamation of prior experiences, emotions, and resulting behaviors [02:21:24].

## Frameworks and models

### The Core 5 Categories Hierarchy
The instructor structures avatar creation into five ordered categories, prioritized by purchase intent and emotional pull:

1. **Category 1: Desires** [00:34:25]
   - Defined as "I want" or "I need" statements found through customer research.
   - Core desires (e.g., status, health) are too broad for initial brand avatars; operators must target surface-level desires (e.g., "I want to brush my teeth faster," "I want my dog to stop pulling").
   - *Validation formula:* If you cannot express it cleanly as "I want X" or "I need Y," it is not a desire [00:40:37].

2. **Category 2: Experiences** [00:42:07]
   - Situations people have gone through or are actively facing.
   - *Situational Experiences:* Environmental circumstances or life events (e.g., "Bedroom gets very bright in the morning," "Just had a baby," "Lived in an apartment with thin walls"). Active situations create urgency; past situations trigger nostalgia [00:42:39], [00:44:07].
   - *Product-Based Experiences:* Instances where a specific solution was attempted and produced an outcome (e.g., "Tried blackout curtains and still woke up tired," "Used a dog harness and it didn't work") [00:44:40].
   - *Validation test:* Ask (1) "Does this describe a circumstance or event?" and (2) "Did we remove the emotion from it?" [01:10:53].

3. **Category 3: Emotions** [01:12:41]
   - How prospects feel about an experience, desire, or behavior; bypasses logical decision-making.
   - *Primary Emotions (Universal):* Joy, Sadness, Fear, Disgust, Anger, Surprise. Universal across humans, but triggers are individual [01:16:30], [01:17:54].
   - *Secondary Emotions (Complex shortcuts):* Anxious, Frustrated, Overwhelmed, Confident, Excited, Stressed, Motivated, Worried, Disappointed, Insecure. Signposts that require digging deeper into underlying causes [01:20:14], [01:21:00].
   - *Validation test:* Ask "Does this describe how a person feels about or in response to something?" If not one of the primary 6, ask "Why are they feeling this?" and "What specifically makes them feel this?" [01:41:27].

4. **Category 4: Behaviors & Habits** [01:43:00]
   - Predictable, recurring action patterns adopted in response to experiences, emotions, or desires (e.g., "Researches everything before buying," "Only shops during sales," "Only walks dog at 5 AM," "Meal preps every Sunday").
   - Strongly tied to self-identity and ego; allows marketers to mirror actions and predict future behaviors [01:45:18], [01:48:00].
   - *Validation test:* Ask "Does this describe what the person does?" (Can be physical or mental, such as overthinking). Gauge strength by asking "How often do they do it?" [02:05:08].

5. **Category 5: Demographics** [02:07:40]
   - Static, factual attributes (Age, Gender, Location, Generation, Race, Language, Religion, Marital Status, Education, Employment, Income, Disabilities, Health Conditions, Height, Weight).
   - Should almost never serve as the starting core avatar unless a product has unique mechanical relevance to that demographic (e.g., melanin-rich skincare, work socks for construction workers) [02:09:25], [02:17:42].
   - *Validation test:* Check if the attribute falls strictly into standard factual demographic buckets [02:20:20].

### The Belief Deconstruction Model
When identifying customer beliefs during research, break them into the underlying Core 5 components [02:22:10]:
- "What happened to make them believe this?" -> **Experience**
- "How does this belief make them feel?" -> **Emotion**
- "What do they do because of this belief?" -> **Behavior**

### Avatar Staging by Revenue Tier
- **Stage 1 ($0-$100k/mo):** Focus on 1 Core Avatar at a time (preferably a single surface-level desire). Build multiple Sub-Avatars combining that desire with specific experiences, emotions, or behaviors. If testing fails after 2–4 weeks, switch to a new desire [02:24:40], [02:26:50].
- **Stage 2 ($100k-$1M/mo):** Maintain 1 Core Avatar while testing deeper sub-avatars and executing higher ad volume. Above $500k/mo, brands can expand to 2–3 total Core Avatars [02:29:02], [02:29:50].
- **Stage 3 ($1M-$10M/mo):** Broaden avatars and run 3–10 Core Avatars simultaneously. Pair avatar relatability with new product mechanisms, educational angles, brand identity, and massive creative volume [02:31:30], [02:32:56].
- **Stage 4 ($10M+/mo):** Max out creative production, global relatability, mechanism sophistication, and brand equity [02:33:40].

## Numbers, benchmarks and rules of thumb
- Avoid combining more than one core surface-level desire when constructing an avatar [00:29:35].
- The more categories combined, the more specific the avatar; fewer categories create broader avatars [00:30:01].
- The broader the avatar, the stronger the product, offer, brand, and marketing skillset must be to generate conversions [00:30:23], [00:36:06].
- Brands generating under $100k/mo should stick to 1 single desire-based core avatar and test sub-avatars for 2 to 4 weeks before considering a pivot [02:24:44], [02:26:50].
- Brands surpassing $500k/mo can expand to 2 to 3 total core avatars [02:29:50].
- Brands scaling between $1M and $10M/mo typically operate across 3 to 10 core avatars [02:32:56].
- Creative volume benchmark: Fast-scaling direct-to-consumer brand Comfrt generates over 10,000 pieces of content per week; even a 1% to 10% relatability rate yields 100 to 1,000 winning variations [02:33:20].

## Exercises and action items assigned
- Review existing customer research and extract surface-level desires by isolating "I want X" or "I need Y" statements [00:40:37].
- Select 1 single surface-level desire as your business's Core Avatar starting point [02:25:20].
- Build multiple Sub-Avatars off that single desire by layering in specific situational/product experiences, emotions, and daily behaviors [02:25:06].
- Audit current ad hooks to replace demographic-first callouts with experience-based or behavior-based hooks (e.g., testing "Do your kids throw tantrums in public?" or "If you never leave the house without snacks..." instead of demographic labeling) [01:54:45].
- Run 2 to 4 weeks of creative testing on sub-avatars under your chosen desire before altering core creative direction [02:26:50].
