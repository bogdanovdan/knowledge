## [00:05:35] Mindset: What To Expect

[frame 00:05:35](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1xAdTJi_HeIcm_SgnoIXiCtkE1Jb1hjWf/view?usp=drivesdk) · `017_00-05-35.png`

*slide*

**On screen:**

Mindset: What To Expect

Expect To Fail
Realistically when you first start testing you're going to suck, and that's okay. It's a skill that can be improved with more reps.

Chase High Intent Reps, Not Perfection
Most beginners don't make it in Ecom because they try to make everything perfect. The more reps you put in the better you get. You only get better through practice.

Intent - Track Everything You Do
Most people test blindly and pray something works. You are relying on LUCK to succeed this way. Track every product and creative you test, and list why you want to test it.

Feedback Loops
When you start you might have a 1% hit rate. So success is guaranteed, but you have to test 100 times. Since you tracked everything, if you review what you're doing your hit rate is going to go up as you test and review.

Success
As you start to learn and get better, you put in enough hours, your product hit rate goes up. Eventually you will see success, but it might take longer than you expected, but it is guaranteed.

## [00:09:45] Key Criteria for Product Success

[frame 00:09:45](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1dnWfDWMx2Uyopu94n7RNsCYM6HIfLM4z/view?usp=drivesdk) · `017_00-09-45.png`

*slide*

**On screen:**

Average Order Value & Profit Margin
Aim for an AOV of $60+ after bundles to ensure profitability, as higher-value products often lead to better margins and overall success.
Aim for a minimum 3x markup on COGs+Shipping price, ideally more. This will give you a huge advantage when you start scaling

Trend Awareness
Keep an eye on emerging trends that resonate with your audience. Staying current helps you identify winning opportunities in the market.
Do a quick Google Trends search on the niche and trend. It doesn't have to be trending up, as long as it's not trending down.

Product Weight
Avoid heavy items that complicate shipping.
If you are a beginner, focus on lightweight products to minimize costs and maximize customer satisfaction during delivery.
If you're a complete beginner don't think about LTV and subscriptions just yet.

The Magnitude of Desire
As mentioned in the dedicated Market Desire video, some products solve stronger desires than others. A product addressing a strong desire can be expensive, require significant effort, and still sell well because the pain of not solving the problem outweighs everything else.

Products that address this:

Strong Desire: I need to lose 30 pounds before my wedding in 3 months
Products that address this:
• High-ticket weight loss coaching programs ($2,000-5,000)
• Specialty meal delivery services ($300-500/month)
• Personal training packages ($1,000-3,000)
• Medical weight loss procedures ($5,000-15,000)

What Affects How Easy & Hard Is It To Make A Product Work

Market Awareness
Also explained in depth in the previous modules. If you didn't watch those, go back. You can't shortcut success.

The higher the stage of awareness, the harder is to make it work.

While possible, as beginners it is a bad idea.

Targeting Solution aware or product aware is a better ideas.

We scaled to our $800k day with product aware ads.

So you don't have to find a product that does something revolutionary.

We're not reinventing the wheel here, find a product that solves something existing better.

Diagram: Eugene Schwartz's Five Stages of Awareness
Columns: Stage | Best approach
• Unaware (People who don't know they have a problem) | Stories, secrets, infotainment
• Problem aware (People who understand the problem but not the solution) | Pain points, benefits of solving the problem
• Solution aware (People who understand the solution but don't know you provide it) | Intro the product that offers the solution
• Product aware (People who know you sell the solution but aren't sure it's the best one for them) | Proof that the product works
• Most aware (People who know your solution is right for them, they just need to know the cost) | Offers, promos, pricing
(Down arrow running through stages)

## [00:18:10] What Affects Product Difficulty: Market Sophistication

[frame 00:18:10](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Ue235UkgGvkBgG42HEokhvdpvssjX9Za/view?usp=drivesdk) · `017_00-18-10.png`

*slide*

**On screen:**

What Affects How Easy & Hard Is It To Make A Product Work

Market Sophistication
The more sophisticated the market, the harder it is to stand out.
You don't need to be new to the market to win; such opportunities are extremely rare. What you do need to know is how to win even in sophisticated markets.
If you cannot create a unique mechanism from the product, or find an underserved avatar, I would avoid it because you don't have a differentiator.

Diagram: Level of Market Sophistication vs Time, Amount and Quality of Buying Experiences (Increasing Skepticism)
• Level 1: Brand New Market / Make any claim
• Level 2: Make a Bigger Claim than your Competitor
• Level 3: Introduce a Big Differentiator that supports the Claim and Convinces the Mind
• Level 4: Create a newer or better Differentiator, or improve on the previous one. "Widget 2.0"
• Level 5: Differentiator burns out. Audience skepticism is at an all time high. Full-on Empathic Marketing is now the only option.
(Adapted from Eugene Schwartz, Breakthrough Advertising)
