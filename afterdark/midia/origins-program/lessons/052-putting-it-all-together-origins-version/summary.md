## Overview

This lesson demonstrates how to combine avatar research, angles, variations, and formats into structured ad testing plans using real campaign examples. Aimed at e-commerce operators in the Origins program, the training walks through the practical differences between the Marksman Method and the Sniper Method across both static images and video creatives. The goal is to enforce thorough preparation so ad concepts have clear intent and customer relevance before entering production.

## Key teachings

- Ad plans must be intentionally structured around avatars and angles before jumping into creative production, rather than rapidly spamming untested ideas [00:00:16].
- When multiple angles or sub-avatars are tested simultaneously within a single concept batch, use the Marksman Method [00:02:54].
- In a Marksman Method test, each creative variation represents a completely different angle or sub-avatar (e.g., Testing 2–3 different angles with 1 creative variation each) [00:03:20].
- When focusing on a single winning angle or sub-avatar, use the Sniper Method to test multiple ways of communicating that single angle [00:05:54].
- Static image ads should almost always use the Marksman Method to discover angle direction quickly, unless optimizing a confirmed winning angle [00:04:04, [00:08:41].
- For video ads, beginners should default to the Sniper Method, selecting one proven or high-priority angle and testing three different hook executions paired with the same hold [00:06:01, [00:07:48].
- If applying the Marksman Method to video creatives, the hooks must be specific to each sub-avatar, while the body ("hold") must remain generic enough to address the core avatar's shared desire [00:09:20, [00:10:19].
- A video hold that is overly specific to one sub-avatar will break congruency when paired with hooks addressing different sub-avatars [00:11:20].
- The ultimate purpose of the Marksman Method is to establish broad direction fast; once Meta favors an angle, switch to the Sniper Method to build deeper, tailored creative experiences [00:11:40].
- Spending up to 90% of your time on avatar research and pre-planning makes execution simple and prevents writer's block during production [00:16:04].

## Frameworks and models

### The 3:2:2 Creative Testing Structure
Meta testing unit consisting of:
- **3 Creatives**: In video, 3 hook variations (first 2–10 seconds) leading into 1 shared hold; in statics, 3 image variations.
- **2 Body Copies**: 2 primary text options.
- **2 Headlines**: 2 headline copy options.

### Ad Plan Setup Components
Every ad concept is broken down into five structured elements:
1. **Concept**: The big idea, format style, or template being leveraged (e.g., "Nutella Ad", "Shift Worker Experience", "Meme Static Ads").
2. **Angle(s) / Sub-Avatar(s)**: The specific customer segment, desire, emotion, or mechanism being highlighted.
3. **Variations**: The exact hook lines, visual copy, or angles assigned to creative 1, 2, and 3.
4. **Format**: The creative design type (e.g., Static Graphic/Design, Meme Style, Subtitles B-Roll).
5. **Testing Method**: Either Marksman Method or Sniper Method.

### Marksman Method vs. Sniper Method
- **Marksman Method**:
  - **Structure**: 1 Concept -> Multiple Angles/Sub-Avatars -> 1 Variation per Angle.
  - **Best Used For**: Static image testing, finding initial creative direction fast.
  - **Video Application**: Specific hook for each angle + generic hold addressing the core avatar's shared mechanism.
- **Sniper Method**:
  - **Structure**: 1 Concept -> 1 Single Angle/Sub-Avatar -> Multiple Variations (Hooks/Executions) of that same angle.
  - **Best Used For**: Video ads, iterating on winners, optimizing specific sub-avatar messaging.

### Hook vs. Hold Video Structure
- **Hook (0:00–0:03 or up to 0:10)**: Calls out *who* needs the product using specific POV callouts, comment stickers, or bridges.
- **Hold (Remaining video length)**: Shows *why* they need it, demonstrating the mechanism and product benefits.

## Numbers, benchmarks and rules of thumb

- **Standard Hook Duration**: The first 2 to 3 seconds of a video ad [00:10:06].
- **Extended Hook / Bridge Duration**: Hook extension up to 3 to 10 seconds before transitioning to the hold [00:09:54].
- **Rule of Thumb for Image Ads**: Image Ads = Marksman Method (3 different angles per batch) [00:12:00].
- **Rule of Thumb for Video Ads**: Video Ads = Sniper Method (1 angle, 3 hook variations) [00:12:00].
- **Effort Allocation Rule**: Spend 90% of your time preparing and planning ads so execution flows naturally [00:16:04].
