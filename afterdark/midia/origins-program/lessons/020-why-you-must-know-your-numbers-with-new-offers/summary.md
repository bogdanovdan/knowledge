## Overview
This lesson explains why e-commerce founders must model profit margins and break-even ROAS before launching sales offers or scaling ad spend. The instructor demonstrates how running discounted offers can inflate top-line revenue and ad spend while sharply reducing actual gross profit. The training is directed at early-stage e-commerce operators who need to set viable target ROAS thresholds and maintain profit upfront rather than pursuing vanity revenue metrics.

## Key teachings
- Offers can act like an addictive drug: while they can increase ROAS from 2x to 4x overnight and lift conversion rates from 1.5% to 2.5%, businesses can become overly reliant on them to acquire customers [00:00:39], [00:01:55].
- Highly sophisticated brands with innovative products (e.g., Carepod) can rely less on heavy discounting, but beginners should still leverage offers to provide value and secure their first cash flow [00:01:28], [00:02:10].
- Top-line revenue and ROAS increases do not automatically mean higher profit; discounting degrades gross margin, which can cause net profit to fall even as revenue scales [00:07:08], [00:10:42].
- Operational costs should not be built into break-even ROAS calculations, but product cost, shipping, and payment processing fees must be included [00:03:12], [00:05:25].
- Always round calculated break-even ROAS upward to create a safety buffer for unaccounted fees [00:03:21], [00:06:29].
- Scaling spend at a historical ROAS target after introducing a discount offer is dangerous; whenever an offer lowers margins, the ROAS target must be recalculated and reset [00:13:05], [00:14:03].
- Bank account cash must be monitored directly rather than relying solely on spreadsheet projections, as real-world secondary expenses always emerge [00:13:51].

## Frameworks and models

### Gross Profit Calculation Model
- `Gross profit margin = (Product price + Shipping charged - Cost of goods - Shipping cost) / (Product price + Shipping charged)` [00:05:20]
- `Gross profit = Total revenue x Gross profit margin - Ad spend` [00:08:16]

### The Delta Method for Resetting ROAS Targets
Used to determine the minimum ROAS required to maintain profitability after launching a discounted offer [00:14:15]:
1. Calculate the delta between the current running ROAS and the current break-even ROAS:  
   `Historical delta = Current ROAS - Current break-even ROAS` (e.g., `2.0 - 1.43 = 0.57`).
2. Add that delta to the *new* break-even ROAS of the discounted offer:  
   `Minimum scaled ROAS target = New break-even ROAS + Historical delta` (e.g., `1.82 + 0.57 = 2.39`).
3. Maintain this new figure as the bare minimum target ROAS to ensure scaled spend generates more profit than the evergreen baseline [00:15:10].

### The "+1 Rule of Thumb" Target ROAS Framework
A quick method to set target ad account ROAS [00:15:33]:
- `Target ROAS = Break-even ROAS + 1.0`
- If performance drops below this target, account health is eroding; if sustained above this target, the business has room to scale spend aggressively [00:16:09].

## Numbers, benchmarks and rules of thumb
- **Addiction benchmark:** Offers can move conversion rates from 1.5% to 2.5% and lift ROAS from 2x to 4x rapidly [00:00:39], [00:01:55].
- **Evergreen baseline scenario:** Spending $100,000/month at 2.0x ROAS with a 1.43 break-even (70% gross profit) yields $200,000 revenue and $40,000 gross profit ($200,000 x 0.70 - $100,000) [00:03:40], [00:08:16].
- **Discount margin erosion scenario:** A 30% discount offer lowers gross profit margin from 70% to 55% (break-even ROAS moves from 1.43 to 1.82). Scaling ad spend to $150,000/month at the same 2.0x ROAS generates $300,000 revenue (a 50% revenue increase) but drops gross profit from $40,000 down to $15,000 ($300,000 x 0.55 - $150,000) [00:03:40], [00:10:42].
- **Small-budget margin sensitivity:** At $10,000 spend, a 70% margin business at 2.0x ROAS nets $4,000. If margins drop to 55%, the ROAS must hit 3.0x to generate $6,500 [00:11:35], [00:12:10].
- **Frequency of review:** Run offer and margin numbers weekly rather than daily [00:13:40].
