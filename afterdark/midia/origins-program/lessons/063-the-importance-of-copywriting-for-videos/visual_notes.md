The Beginner Mistake: Talking about how great your product is, how long you've been in business, your awards, and your mission.

#3 - Write How People Talk, Not How They Read
The Beginner Mistake: Writing in "proper" formal English like you learned in school.
The Truth: Copy should sound like a conversation. Use contractions. Short sentences. Start sentences with "And" or "But." Write like you're talking to a friend.
The Test: Read your copy out loud. If it sounds weird or stiff, rewrite it.
Pro tip: Use the https://hemingwayapp.com/ to confirm your writing is at a 6th grade level or below (can ask Claude to rewrite it if needed).

- ✓ "This tells your body to burn fat instead of storing it"

#4 - Copy is never too long... Only too boring.
The Beginner Mistake: "People won't read long copy" - so they write short, incomplete copy.
The Truth: People who are interested in what you're selling will read EVERYTHING, as long as it's engaging.

[frame 00:15:00](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1ucwztOAJfz38MLJs-elXmJ9ZieSVTyoX/view?usp=drivesdk) · `063_00-15-00.png`

#6 - Research is 80% of the Work
What to Research:
• Customer reviews (their exact language)
• Competitor claims (what's already been said)
• Amazon Q&A sections (their questions = your objections)
• Reddit/Facebook groups (raw, unfiltered pain points)
• Product mechanism (understand it deeply)
The Rule: Spend 2-3 hours researching for every 1 hour writing.

⭐ #7 - The Daily Practice (Joe Schriefer)
If you do this for 90 days and you'll be better than 90% of copywriters.
1. Read a piece of copy (active reading - analyze the subtext - what is being communicated?)
2. Write a page of copy (doesn't have to be for an ad, can be for a landing page, a tweet, anything.)
3. Come up with an idea (keep asking "what if...")
a. "What if I approached this project from a completely different angle?"
b. "Why did this ad stop me? What's the hook pattern?"
c. "How would I improve this ad if it was mine?"
d. "Why did I just buy this? What was the trigger?"
e. "What would make me buy this faster?"
f. "I just got frustrated by X - that's a pain point someone could sell into"
g. "Everyone in this niche says X - what if we said Y instead?"

THE ROLE OF COPY LENGTH
Schwartz's Awareness-Based Length Rules:
Awareness Level | Copy Length | Why
Most Aware | Short | Just need the deal
Product Aware | Medium | Need differentiation
Solution Aware | Long | Need education on YOUR solution
Problem Aware | Very Long | Need problem + solution education

The Principle: The less they know, the more you must teach. The more you must teach, the longer your copy.

THE 5 STAGES OF AWARENESS
[Chevron arrows pointing left to right across stages]
• Unaware: They aren't aware of their desire or their need to solve the problem—or they won't honestly admit it.
• Problem Aware: They know they have a problem to solve, but aren't aware of any specific solutions.
• Solution Aware: They know there is a solution to their problem, but they don't know of any specific products to solve it.

## [00:42:05] Daily Copywriting Practice Routine by Joe Schriefer

[frame 00:42:05](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Qx0PUO9A2XiaNxMeMJQli1Q4qxbi09JW/view?usp=drivesdk) · `063_00-42-05.png`

**On screen:**

⭐ #7 - The Daily Practice (Joe Schriefer)
If you do this for 90 days and you'll be better than 90% of copywriters.
1. Read a piece of copy (active reading - analyze the subtext - what is being communicated?)
2. Write a page of copy (doesn't have to be for an ad, can be for a landing page, a tweet, anything.)
3. Come up with an idea (keep asking "what if...")
a. "What if I approached this project from a completely different angle?"
b. "Why did this ad stop me? What's the hook pattern?"
c. "How would I improve this ad if it was mine?"
d. "Why did I just buy this? What was the trigger?"
e. "What would make me buy this faster?"
f. "I just got frustrated by X - that's a pain point someone could sell into"
g. "Everyone in this niche says X - what if we said Y instead?"
h. "Nobody's addressing Z objection - what if we led with that?"
Pro Tip: Keep a notes app on your laptop/phone (Apple Notes, Google Keep, Notion) Create a "Copy Ideas" section, hooks that stopped my scroll, etc.
